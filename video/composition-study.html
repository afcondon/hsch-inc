III · READING ORDER — HOW THE EYE TRAVELS, WHERE IT STOPS

Three passes, five stops — and a rule about who does the revealing. The traversal was declared open for round 2; the snake is kept on merit, for two reasons that are geometry rather than habit. First, pass 2's reversal is what places beat 9 vertically above beat 15 — the ghost's dashed drop-line exists because of the fold. Second, the strands run fan → under the bar → sweep → chips as one unbroken gesture; any carriage return would cut it exactly where it is the picture. The cost of the snake — pass 2 reading against text direction — is paid differently now (see the pass-two note below).

Shared frames, resolved by fade. Round 2 gave every beat a 1920px floor and it was the wrong fix: the stage swelled to 14400, frames were marooned in cream, and the pull-back died. Round 3 packs by TIME instead of space: three pairs of short adjacent beats share a camera frame, and within each the camera holds while the earlier beat's copy fades out and the next fades in. Furniture — spine, dip, squares, washes, the placard — is permanent; only copy swaps. Frame A (fx 0, pass 1): beat 1 resolves, then beat 2. Frame B (fx 1682, pass 1): beat 3's dip and quote resolve, then beat 4. Frame F (fx 5760, pass 2): beat 8's cost structure resolves east of centre, fades down, and beat 9's tilted placard fades UP in the west half — the objection interrupts a resting frame, which is better staging than the objection owning a private one.

The eye enters at the diamond and the first bloom, top-left, and runs the spine east through beats 1–5. Beat 3's SQL dip — the bypass in miniature — keeps its own moment inside frame B even though it now shares the picture. Ochre enters at 5 as two slabs: the two costs, stacked toward the wall's foundation.

Stop one: the wall. The first full-ink mass on the surface. The pigment pools and darkens against its west face — thirty years of accumulation, held. Beat 6's tradition runs UNDER it as a thin one-way tunnel that dead-ends: the same shape as the SQL dip, deliberately — a specialist language embedded in a general flow.

The first turn. Beat 7 breaches the wall — the chunk is out, the two ochre slabs are struck through, and the flood is the largest wash in the piece. Its momentum carries the eye off the east edge and DOWN: the turn is not travel, it is the event still happening. The cascade lands in a pool at the head of pass 2 — and because frame E and frame F both sit at fx 5760, the camera's drop is purely vertical. The same is true of the west turn (frames I and J both at fx 0): both folds are now camera drops with no horizontal component, which is the strongest possible claim that the turns land on events, not travel.

The pass-two note. The camera travels east→west; the text still sets left-to-right. Round 2 resolves this by taking reveal away from the camera: each beat's pictorial anchor (the glyph, the placard, the boxes, the fan) sits at the east side of its station, so travel introduces the beat mark-first; the copy is right-aligned, so its hard edge resolves toward the direction of travel; and at animation time the copy fades in whole once the camera rests — no sentence is ever spooled in backwards. In the still, this reads as: picture right, copy left, all copy flush right.

Stop two: the placard. Beat 9 is the only tilted knockout slab on the surface, so it arrests the eye exactly as the objection arrests the argument. Its deferral is written on it — ANSWERED AT 15 ↓ — and a dashed drop-line falls from it toward the third pass. Then stop three: the fan — the first colour beyond blue and ochre anywhere in the piece, held back for eleven beats so that its arrival IS the information. The strands pass under beat 12's prussian bar — one typed layer over the top, literally — and then, west of the bar, they gather: five lanes narrow into a cable, the typed-layer line and the spine close alongside, and the whole bundle changes direction together down the west edge. Second turn, second event — one rope, not seven laps.

Stop four: the chips. The cable unbraids eastward into the chip column: two solid (JavaScript, the BEAM — production for years), three ringed (Julia, Go, Python — this series). The fold reverses strand order through the turn, which is why production lands on top without a single crossing. Pass three then runs east through the evidence — beat 14's number and its unit chart, one mark per project, labelled so it reads as a count rather than texture — to stop five: the close. The ghost of 9 hangs top-right of beat 15's frame on its drop-line — same shape, 12% ink — and is answered in place. No camera returns anywhere. The spine's final arrow lands on the ochre full stop of the wordmark: the argument terminates in the price, and the price is now a dot.

The frame audit. Round 1 was judged on its fit-to-width view and passed; judged frame-by-frame it failed four ways. Round 2 over-corrected with a width floor and the frames went hollow. Round 3 is composed as thirteen camera stops — A 0 · B 1682 · C 3096 · D 4359 · E 5760 / F 5760 · G 3251 · H 1460 · I 0 / J 0 · K 2434 · L 4560 · M 5760 — three of them shared by a fade pair, two of them beat 15's double rest. The exporter (`composition-export.html`) is the instrument of record.

IV · WHAT DID NOT FIT, AND WHAT THE STUDY FOUND

  1. The 1920 floor is out; the stage is 7680 × 3240 (2.37 : 1 — the scope ratio). A full pull-back now shows the surface filling 75% of frame height, so the closing retreat is worth landing on again. Station width is duration-weighted above a 500px minimum — enough that a station's furniture cannot collide with its neighbour's, deliberately not enough to guarantee a private frame.
  2. Three fade pairs are part of the design, not an animation detail. Beats 1→2 (frame A, fx 0), 3→4 (frame B, fx 1682) and 8→9 (frame F, fx 5760) share a camera frame; within each, the earlier beat's copy fades out before the next fades in, furniture persisting throughout. In the still both members of a pair are set side by side so the layout can be judged; in the animation they are never both at full strength. A bonus of the repack: both fold turns became purely vertical camera drops (E→F holds fx 5760; I→J holds fx 0).
  3. Reveal-by-fade is also pass 2's answer, and it is an animation-time fact. The still can show the layout half of the fix (right-aligned copy, anchors east) but not the temporal half (copy fading in whole once the camera rests). Anyone judging pass 2 from the still alone should read the pass-two note first.
  4. Beat 15 is six sub-beats and the static shows its end state. Its station is over a frame and a half wide, and the camera rests twice: L (fx 4560) on the answer with the ghost top-right, M (fx 5760) on the wordmark. All six lines are set, but at animation time they must reveal sequentially in place.
  5. Deliberately left out: the .NET aside (the deck cut it); Rust demoted to small print at 13 per the deck's reordering; the pandoc quote trimmed to its clause; the asterisk graphic (settled 2026-08-04: the real take says it correctly).
  6. Colour discipline is checkable. Ochre appears exactly three times (the two costs, their struck ghosts, the full stop). The five runtime hues appear exactly three times (fan, chips, poster ribbons). If either count is ever wrong, something has decorated.
  7. Frame M stays corner-weighted, deliberately (round 4). As the poster's signature the wordmark belongs bottom-right; as a held camera stop it is off-balance — and the resolution is temporal, not spatial. M is not a held stop: the pull-back begins as the wordmark lands, and its dwell is the retreat itself, which re-frames the same corner into the poster geometry (mass upper-left, wordmark lower-right). Filling M's upper-left to balance the still would pollute the surface's quiet zone and dilute the pull-back's reveal. Carried to the animation brief.
  8. Open question, carried over. The fold reverses strand order through the west turn, so the fan reads PYTHON-first and the chips read JAVASCRIPT-first. Still considered a feature — the chips are where order carries meaning — but if fan order should also be canonical, the strands must cross somewhere, staged as a braid.
