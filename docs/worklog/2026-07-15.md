- **Bayesian layer verdicts**: Stage C (behavioral likelihoods) is
  SOMEDAY — with toy players the policy model is self-referential. But
  the EV decomposition survived: "a rival is already certain" is
  computable from the worlds set alone (their notebook is deterministic
  inside each world); "becomes certain later" irreducibly needs policy
  models. Andrew called the build: a probabilistic Watcher-Watcher.
- **The bettor built** (kibitzer a5c3f2c + 710f460): smt/bettor.py →
  /bettor.html. Uniform posterior = world counting; hazard = share of
  survivors where some rival's view (exact hand + shows-to-them,
  canonically resolved per world) pins the envelope — semantic, all
  within same-hand groups, no engine replays. Two-clause rule: bet at
  P ≥ 0.9 or when forced (P ≥ 0.5 and P > 1 − hazard). Trace shows
  gained a `to` field; example CLI gained a `traces` batch mode;
  validate mode runs parallel (--jobs).

**The finding (200-game silent run)**: 122 bets / 72 correct / 78
abstains, calibration honest (z = −1.1) — but **no confident bet ever
preceded proof**. The posterior snaps rather than climbs (constraints
kill triples in blocks); the bettor's entire edge is the forced
clause: 103 priced coin flips (stated 57%, realised 51%), mean lead 4
events, best 27. EV over proof literally: not foresight — knowing when
the race is lost and taking the odds instead of nothing. Featured seed
11 (wakes at 82% into an 82% hazard, bets immediately, correct, nine
events early). Bonus exhibit: seed 33's hazard plateaus at 0.5 for ~40
events before the deductive winner moves — the completeness gap on the
player side.

## Evening addendum: the JTMS sketch session happened same-day

Queued item 1 above ran this evening rather than waiting. Outcome:
**purescript-jtms is born** — repo at afc-work root, Marginalia #253
(root project, see-also links to kibitzer/Harmonia/ShapedSteer), and
`docs/library-sketch.md` as the kickoff artifact.

The session's load-bearing realisations, now in the sketch:

- **Kibitzer already made the Faubion move.** Andrew's intuition was
  a "Free From Tree"-style lift of the propagation engine; the answer
  is that the free structure is already there — `Justification` is
  the defunctionalised rule application (the instruction functor of
  *inference*, not sequencing), and `addFact`'s first-wins dedup is
  hash-consing, which is what makes the free tree a DAG. The library
  work is abstracting the signature, not discovering the structure.
- **Free enters at rule bodies, not the engine**: free applicative
  over a Match functor → statically extractable read-sets → semi-naive
  evaluation + stratification + the rule-wiring diagram from one
  interpretation. `saturate` itself is a lattice fixpoint (Datalog/
  propagator tradition) and shouldn't be free-anything.
- **Extensions costed** (utility / impl / ease-of-use): windowed rules
  in v1 (timestamps don't break monotonicity, forgetting does),
  stratified negation is core not optional (Cluedo needs it),
  retraction-by-replay is free, Doyle's full non-monotonic system is
  sequenced last and **gates the public name** — Andrew: don't call it
  a JTMS publicly unless it IS one; working name purescript-jtms,
  rename before publication, Baskerville the leading candidate.
- **Prior-art check**: Kmett's propagators/guanxi are research
  sketches, Harding's holmes shipped — but the whole lineage aims
  provenance at search (nogoods for CDCL). Nobody ships the
  derivation DAG as the user-facing product. The niche is open.

### And then we built it (late evening — the library exists)

Andrew: "we have a lot of context here — could take a first shot at
coding this." Four working sessions later, purescript-jtms is a real
spago workspace: **9 commits, 21 tests green, zero warnings, pushed to
github.com/afcondon/purescript-jtms (private — the naming gate says
no public "JTMS" until it is one)**.

What got built, in order:

1. **Kernel v0** (`jtms-core`): Jtms.Kernel (Why = Axiom | ByRule,
   hash-consed feed whose Map claim FactId index doubles as the
   premise lookup, acyclicity by construction), Jtms.Engine (prim-form
   rules, naive saturation with a residue hook, `learn` as the stream
   fold), Jtms.Explain (`explain` is a single backward scan — no
   recursion, the acyclicity invariant does the work; depthOf gives
   the cascade stagger order). Kernel laws tested on a toy
   reachability client.
2. **The Sudoku client** (`jtms-sudoku`): prune facts first-class
   (`Not cell digit` with derivations), four singles-tier rules,
   soundness pinned against the generating pattern grid, honest stall
   on an underdetermined puzzle. War story: hand-transcribed fixtures
   produced contradictory givens TWICE — the solver faithfully derived
   garbage from false axioms. Fixtures are now generated
   (Sudoku.Fixtures) and the client grew a `consistent` check.
3. **The rule eDSL** (Jtms.Rule): RuleM = KB-reader × nondeterminism ×
   premise/anchor-writer. `matches`/`require` test AND cite — the
   derivation writes itself; forgotten citations are now
   unrepresentable in eDSL rules. All four Sudoku rules rewrote into
   it (naked single = four lines) with every proof-shape test
   unchanged and a byte-identical demo transcript. Analyzability
   (free-applicative read-sets) deliberately deferred until the
   semi-naive engine exists to consume it.
4. **All-justifications recording**: addFact appends genuinely-new
   rederivations to alsoWhy; justificationsFor reads them back. The
   stability law caught a real design decision: derivation identity is
   CONTENT (rule + premises + anchors), not source — and acyclicity
   belongs to the primary proof only (alternatives may cite later
   facts; well-foundedness of alternatives is where ATMS starts).
5. **The explain page** (Sudoku.Page): one deduction's DAG as a
   Swiss-style HTML page, depth-layered, rule-coloured, +n
   rederivability badges, proof footprint on the grid. Forced a third
   fixture: diagonalsPuzzle proved too parallel (all proofs exactly 17
   facts), so chainPuzzle (both diagonals + subdiagonal) provides
   genuine chains — the rendered proof runs five layers:
   givens → eliminations → r8c2=7 → r8c8≠7 → r8c8=4.
